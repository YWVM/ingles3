1️⃣  How it changes and what it's used for

▼

What is it used for? The verb to be is the most important verb in English. It is used to express:

  • Identity, characteristics and professions (like Spanish ser)
  • States, emotions and location (like Spanish estar)
  • Forming continuous tenses (I am studying) and the passive voice (It was made)

🔄 Conjugation (present tense)

💡 Tip: To be covers both Spanish verbs «ser» and «estar». Only the form changes depending on the pronoun!

2️⃣  Negative sentences (am not, isn't, aren't)

▼

How are they formed? Just add not after the verb to be:

Subject + to be + NOT + complement

PronounFull negativeContracted negative
II am not …I'm not …
He / She / ItHe is not …He isn't …
You / We / TheyThey are not …They aren't …
💡 Note: With I the contraction is I'm not (there is no "amn't"). The form ain't is informal and avoided in standard English.
Affirmative → Negative
  • I am happy. → I am not happy. / I'm not happy.
    Soy feliz. → No soy feliz.
  • She is tired. → She isn't tired.
    Ella está cansada. → Ella no está cansada.
  • He is at home. → He is not at home.
    Él está en casa. → Él no está en casa.
  • They are teachers. → They aren't teachers.
    Ellos son profesores. → Ellos no son profesores.
  • We are late. → We are not late.
    Estamos tarde. → No estamos tarde.
  • You are my friend. → You aren't my friend.
    Eres mi amigo. → No eres mi amigo.
📁

3️⃣  Interrogative sentences (Are you…? Is he…?)

▼

How are they formed? Invert the word order: the verb to be goes before the subject.

TO BE + subject + complement + ?

PronounQuestionShort answer
IAm I …?Yes, you are. / No, you're not.
YouAre you …?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
HeIs he …?Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
SheIs she …?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
ItIs it …?Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
WeAre we …?Yes, we are. / No, we aren't.
TheyAre they …?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
Affirmative → Interrogative
  • You are a doctor. → Are you a doctor?
    Eres médico. → ¿Eres médico?
  • He is 20 years old. → Is he 20 years old?
    Él tiene 20 años. → ¿Él tiene 20 años?
  • She is at home. → Is she at home?
    Ella está en casa. → ¿Ella está en casa?
  • They are students. → Are they students?
    Ellos son estudiantes. → ¿Ellos son estudiantes?
  • We are late. → Are we late?
    Estamos tarde. → ¿Estamos tarde?
💡 With question words (What, Where, How…): the question word comes first, then to be, then the subject.
Where is she? — ¿Dónde está ella?
How are you? — ¿Cómo estás?
What is your name? — ¿Cuál es tu nombre?

4️⃣  Main uses of the verb

▼
🪪 1. Identity  (= Spanish «ser»)
  • I am Ana.  Yo soy Ana.
  • This is my brother.  Este es mi hermano.
  • She is my teacher.  Ella es mi profesora.
😮‍💨 2. State / condition  (= Spanish «estar»)
  • She is tired.  Ella está cansada.
  • I am happy today.  Estoy feliz hoy.
  • They are hungry.  Ellos tienen hambre.
📍 3. Location  (= Spanish «estar»)
  • We are at home.  Estamos en casa.
  • The book is on the table.  El libro está sobre la mesa.
  • He is in the classroom.  Él está en el salón de clases.
🎂 4. Age
  • He is 20 years old.  Él tiene 20 años.
  • I am 15.  Yo tengo 15 años.
  • How old are you?  ¿Cuántos años tienes?
💼 5. Profession  (= Spanish «ser»)
  • They are teachers.  Ellos son profesores.
  • My mother is a nurse.  Mi madre es enfermera.
  • I am an engineer.  Yo soy ingeniero.
💡 Remember: In English you don't say "I have 20 years". Always use to be for age: He is 20 years old.
📁

5️⃣  Wrap-up: summary + mini-exercise

▼

⚡ Quick summary

  • ✅ I → am  |  He/She/It → is  |  You/We/They → are
  • 🚫 Negative: to be + not (isn't, aren't, I'm not)
  • ❓ Interrogative: invert the order → Are you…? Is he…?
  • 🪪 Used for: identity, state, location, age and profession

✏️ Mini-exercise: fill in with am / is / are

  • 1. I   a student.
